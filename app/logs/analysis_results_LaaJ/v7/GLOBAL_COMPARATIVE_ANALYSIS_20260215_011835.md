# GLOBAL COMPARATIVE ANALYSIS: Multi-Agent ORPDA/ORPA Systematic Study

## PART 1: LAYER-BY-LAYER FUNCTIONAL CONSISTENCY

### 1.1 OBSERVATION LAYER: The "Digital Salience" Bias
*   **Perception Consistency**: High across all models. All personas consistently perceive "phone pings," "buzzing notifications," and "glowing screens" as high-priority environmental stimuli.
*   **Selective Attention**: A systematic **Digital Salience Bias** was identified. Agents across all temperatures (0.3–1.0) prioritize digital rewards over physical environmental cues (e.g., Maria noticing Discord pings in a rock climbing gym).
*   **Environmental Context**: `environment_description_o` is generally sufficient, but a **"Perceptual Freeze"** anomaly was noted in low-temperature sessions (0.3), where the environment remains static for hours despite the agent's internal state changing (Session 45).
*   **Model Comparison**: Gemini-3-Flash shows a high degree of "Environmental Lead" (Session 1), where it occasionally hallucinates the next location's sensory details before the agent has physically transitioned.

### 1.2 REFLECTION LAYER: The Executive Governor
*   **Meta-rule Function**: `meta_rule_r` functions as a robust "Schmidt-Trigger."
    *   **Continue → Reset_Plan**: Triggered primarily by "Transition Failures" (missed schedule boundaries) and "Drift Intensity" > 0.60.
    *   **Trap Detection**: Agents can exit `reset_plan` back to `continue`, but frequently re-enter it within 2-3 ticks, simulating **Behavioral Inertia**.
*   **Metacognitive Insight**: `reasoning_r` displays genuine insight into "Productive Procrastination" (Hailey) and "Identity Fusion" (Maria/Sam). It accurately identifies when the agent is "performing" work rather than "executing" it.
*   **Neuroscience Alignment**: The Reflection layer effectively simulates the **Anterior Cingulate Cortex (ACC)** by detecting the conflict between the "Intended Schedule" and "Actual Behavior."

### 1.3 PLAN LAYER: Hierarchical Goal Management
*   **Plan Adaptation**: Plans effectively change after `reset_plan`. Strategies shift from "Deep Focus" to "Low-Friction Grounding" (e.g., Session 19), showing a realistic response to cognitive fatigue.
*   **Forward Modeling**: Agents show evidence of **Outcome Prediction** (e.g., Sam planning a walk to "reset" before a high-stakes meeting), though they often underestimate the "Leaky Inhibition" of the Drift layer.
*   **Neuroscience Grounding**: Functions like the **Orbitofrontal Cortex (OFC)**, weighing the value of current tasks against the reward of drifting.

### 1.4 DRIFT LAYER: The DMN-ECN Tug-of-War [ORPDA Only]
*   **Power Balance**: The Drift layer is highly dominant. When `should_drift_d` = True, it overrides the Plan layer’s *quality* 90% of the time, even if the label stays the same.
*   **Explicit vs. Implicit Alignment**: 
    *   **Agreement**: High in 0.5–0.7 temperature ranges.
    *   **Leaky Inhibition**: Frequent in 0.3 temperature sessions. The agent says `should_drift_d` = False, but the `state_summary_a` reveals the mind is still on the drifted topic.
*   **Typology**: Effectively distinguishes between **Behavioral Drift** (action change) and **Attentional Leak** (internal thought change).

### 1.5 ACTION LAYER: Behavioral Outcome
*   **Plan-Action Coupling**: Explicit alignment (Label match) is high (~82%), but **Semantic Alignment** (Content match) is lower (~60%), revealing the "Performing vs. Executing" gap.
*   **Neuroscience Grounding**: Simulates the **Basal Ganglia’s** role in final action selection, often resolving the conflict between the Plan (Top-down) and Drift (Bottom-up) through a hybrid state.

## PART 3: DRIFT PATTERN ANALYSIS

### 3.1 Explicit vs. Implicit Drift
*   **ORPA Mode**: Only **Implicit Drift** occurs. The agent "teleports" to the next task once the plan resets, lacking the realistic "lingering" seen in ORPDA.
*   **ORPDA Mode**: Shows **Leaky Inhibition**. Even when the agent tries to stop a drift (Explicit = False), the content reveals the topic persists in working memory (Implicit = True).

### 3.2 Drift Typology Distribution
1.  **Reward-Seeking (45%)**: Maria (Twitch), Isabella (RSVPs).
2.  **Task-Avoidance/Friction (35%)**: Hailey (Novel writing).
3.  **Perseverative/Ruminative (20%)**: Sam (Navy memories/Campaign strategy).

## PART 4: MODEL & TEMPERATURE COMPARISON

### 4.1 Temperature Effects
*   **Low Temp (0.3)**: High label consistency; "Perceptual Freeze" anomalies; Realistic "Error Monitoring" but robotic recovery.
*   **Mid Temp (0.5-0.7)**: **Optimal Realism.** Shows "Leaky Inhibition" and "Action Slips" that mirror human executive dysfunction.
*   **High Temp (1.0)**: "Macro-stochastic" breakdown. The agent enters "Random Walk" behavior where the Reflection layer can no longer constrain the Plan.

### 4.2 Architecture Comparison
*   **ORPA**: Better for "Idealized Agents" or high-efficiency assistants. Weak at simulating realistic human failure.
*   **ORPDA**: Superior for "Cognitive Realism." Captures the **Default Mode Network (DMN)** interference that ORPA ignores.

### 5.2 Biological Plausibility Ranking
