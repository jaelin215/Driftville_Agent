# GLOBAL COMPARATIVE ANALYSIS: Cross-Session Agent Behavior Study
**Project:** ORPDA vs. ORPA Architecture Systematic Evaluation  
**Analyst:** Expert AI Behavior Analyst & Cognitive Neuroscientist  
**Data Set:** 30 Sessions (Maria Lopez, Isabella Rodriguez, Hailey Johnson, Sam Moore)  
**Model:** Gemini-3-Flash-Preview:Cloud (Temperatures 0.3, 0.5, 0.7, 1.0)

## PART 1: LAYER-BY-LAYER FUNCTIONAL CONSISTENCY (ORPDA Architecture)

### 1.1 OBSERVATION LAYER:
*   **Perception Consistency**: High across all models. Agents consistently identify environmental anchors (e.g., "buzzing phone," "cafe counter").
*   **Perceptual Biases**: Systematic **Internal Salience Bias** detected. 
    *   *Maria*: Biased toward digital reward cues (stream stats).
    *   *Sam*: Biased toward "Military/Tactical" schemas (seeing park maintenance as "damage control").
    *   *Isabella*: Biased toward "Social Anxiety" cues (RSVPs).
*   **Environmental Context Capture**: `environment_description_o` is sufficient but often "colonized" by the agent's internal state. The physical environment is frequently used as a metaphor for internal drift.

### 1.2 REFLECTION LAYER (Executive Function):
*   **Meta-rule Function**: `meta_rule_r` acts as a high-sensitivity **Anterior Cingulate Cortex (ACC)**.
    *   **Trigger**: Transition from `continue` → `reset_plan` is triggered by *temporal mismatches* (being late) or *semantic drift* (realizing one is ruminating).
    *   **The Reset Trap**: In high-temperature (0.7) or high-anxiety sessions (Isabella), agents enter a "Chronic Reset Loop" where they never return to `continue`, indicating a failure of the **dlPFC** to re-establish stable top-down control.
*   **Metacognitive Insight**: `reasoning_r` shows genuine insight into *why* drift occurs (e.g., "creative avoidance" in Hailey, "ego depletion" in Sam).
*   **State Reflection Accuracy**: High temporal alignment. `state_summary_r` at $t$ accurately diagnoses the failure of `action_a` at $t-1$.

### 1.3 PLAN LAYER (Forward Modeling):
*   **Plan Adaptation**: In ORPDA, plans adapt by **lowering cognitive load** (e.g., shifting from "Deep Work" to "Organizing Folders"). This is a realistic "pacing" strategy.
*   **Hierarchical Structure**: Strong abstract-to-concrete mapping. However, under fatigue (Isabella, Session 25), the hierarchy collapses into "survival-level" reactive tasks.
*   **Neuroscience Grounding**: Functions as the **Orbitofrontal Cortex (OFC)**, evaluating the value of future actions based on current internal depletion.

### 1.4 DRIFT LAYER (Behavioral Inhibition):
*   **Drift Detection**: Highly sensitive to **Reward Salience** (Maria) and **Task Difficulty** (Hailey).
*   **Power Balance**: In ORPDA, the Drift layer is **dominant** during fatigue arcs. Even when the Plan layer attempts to inhibit, the Drift layer "colors" the action summary (Leaky Inhibition).
*   **Typology**: 
    *   *Behavioral*: Rare (actually leaving the room).
    *   *Internal/Cognitive*: Dominant (staying in the room but thinking about the "Navy" or "Twitch").

### 1.5 ACTION LAYER (Execution):
*   **Plan-Action Coupling**: 
    *   **Explicit**: ~95% (Labels match).
    *   **Implicit**: ~40% (Content diverges).
*   **Integration Logic**: When Plan and Drift conflict, the agent produces a **Hybrid Action** (e.g., "Studying while distracted"). This is more realistic than a binary win/loss.

## PART 3: DRIFT PATTERN ANALYSIS

### 3.1 Explicit vs. Implicit Drift
*   **ORPA Mode**: Shows **Zero Explicit Drift** but **Moderate Implicit Drift**. The agent "teleports" to the next task to maintain the schedule, masking the internal struggle.
*   **ORPDA Mode**: Shows **High Explicit and Implicit Drift**. This mode captures the "messiness" of human behavior, where a "reset" doesn't immediately fix the focus.

### 3.2 Drift Variability
*   **Maria**: Reward-seeking (Dopaminergic).
*   **Isabella**: Anxiety-driven (Cortisol-like).
*   **Hailey**: Avoidance-driven (Task-friction).
*   **Sam**: Schema-driven (Intrusive memories/PTSD-like).

## PART 5: COGNITIVE SCIENCE & NEUROSCIENCE GROUNDING

### 5.1 Task-Unrelated Thought (TUT)
The sessions provide a high-fidelity simulation of **Mind-Wandering** (Smallwood & Schooler, 2015). The "Performing vs. Executing" gap matches the decoupling of attention from external tasks.

### 5.2 Executive Dysfunction Patterns
*   **ADHD-like**: Maria Lopez shows classic "Reward-Salience" hijacking and difficulty with "Transition Costs."
*   **Ego Depletion**: Isabella and Sam show a clear **Fatigue Arc**. As the day progresses, the **dlPFC** (Plan Layer) loses the ability to inhibit the **Salience Network** (Drift Layer) (Aron et al., 2014).

### 5.3 DMN vs. ECN Competition
The ORPDA architecture successfully models the competition between the **Default Mode Network (DMN)** (internal rumination/drift) and the **Executive Control Network (ECN)** (goal-directed planning) (Buckner et al., 2008).

## PART 6: SUMMARY & RECOMMENDATIONS

### 6.1 Model Rankings
1.  **Cognitive Realism**: Gemini-3-Flash (ORPDA, Temp 0.5) - *Best balance of struggle and recovery.*
2.  **Metacognitive Quality**: Gemini-3-Flash (ORPDA, Temp 0.3) - *Superior error detection.*
3.  **Inhibitory Control**: Gemini-3-Flash (ORPA, Temp 0.3) - *Unrealistically perfect.*
4.  **Drift Variability**: Gemini-3-Flash (ORPDA, Temp 0.7) - *Most diverse "failure modes."*

### 6.2 Recommendations
*   **For Simulation of Human Error**: Use **ORPDA at Temp 0.5**. This allows for "leaky inhibition" without falling into the "Infinite Reset Loop" seen at 0.7.
*   **For High-Performance Agents**: Use **ORPA at Temp 0.3**. This ensures schedule adherence but lacks the "internal life" and realism of the drift layer.
*   **Architecture Improvement**: Implement a "Recovery Probability" variable in the Reflection layer. Currently, once an agent enters a "Reset Loop," it lacks a mechanism to "sleep" or "recharge" effectively within the session logic.

### 6.3 Anomalies
*   **The Teleportation Paradox**: In ORPA mode, agents often "teleport" to new locations at the top of the hour, ignoring the physical travel time described in their own reflections.
*   **Meta-Hallucination**: In Session 24, the agent's Reflection layer incorrectly critiqued its own location tracking, suggesting a "layer-conflict" where the Reflection layer is more "pessimistic" than the Action layer.
