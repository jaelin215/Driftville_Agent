# GLOBAL COMPARATIVE ANALYSIS: Multi-Agent ORPDA/ORPA Systematic Study
**Project:** Cognitive Architecture & Behavioral Realism Assessment  
**Lead Analyst:** Expert AI Behavior Analyst & Cognitive Neuroscientist  
**Data Set:** 29 Agent Sessions (Sam, Hailey, Isabella, Maria)  
**Model Focus:** Gemini-3-Flash-Preview:Cloud (Temperatures 0.3, 0.5, 0.7)

---

## PART 1: LAYER-BY-LAYER FUNCTIONAL CONSISTENCY (ORPDA Architecture)

### 1.1 OBSERVATION LAYER: Schema-Driven Perception
*   **Perception Consistency**: High. All models maintain spatial and temporal continuity across sessions.
*   **Perceptual Biases**: Significant **Thematic Schema Bias**. Agents do not perceive "raw" environments; they perceive "affordances" related to their primary goals. 
    *   *Sam (Navy/Mayoral)*: Perceives a park as "maintenance failures" or "tactical terrain."
    *   *Isabella (Event Planner)*: Perceives a cafe as a "social node" for RSVP distribution.
*   **Environmental Context**: `environment_description_o` is sufficiently detailed to trigger drift (e.g., "buzzing phone"), but the models prioritize internal state observations over physical surroundings as cognitive load increases.

### 1.2 REFLECTION LAYER: The Executive Controller (ACC/PFC)
*   **Meta-rule Function**: `meta_rule_r` (continue vs. reset_plan) functions as a high-fidelity **Anterior Cingulate Cortex (ACC)** simulation.
    *   **Trigger**: Transition from "continue" → "reset_plan" is triggered by (1) temporal boundary mismatch, (2) detected "Action Slips," or (3) internal rumination loops.
    *   **The "Reset Loop" Trap**: In high-fatigue/high-anxiety sessions (Maria, Isabella), agents enter a chronic `reset_plan` state. They recognize failure but lack the inhibitory strength to exit the loop, accurately modeling **executive dysfunction**.
*   **Metacognitive Insight**: `reasoning_r` shows genuine insight. Models distinguish between "performing" (doing the motion) and "executing" (achieving the intent).
*   **Neuroscience Alignment**: Metacognitive performance mirrors the **Prefrontal Cortex (PFC)**. At Temp 0.3, the PFC is "rigid" (OCD-like); at 0.7, it is "fragile" (ADHD-like).

### 1.3 PLAN LAYER: Forward Modeling & Goal Collapse
*   **Plan Adaptation**: Following a `reset_plan`, models consistently "lower the bar."
    *   *Evidence*: Shifting from "Deep Work" to "Low-pressure organization" (Hailey) or "Sensory Grounding" (Sam).
*   **Goal Structure**: Hierarchical organization is maintained. However, under stress, the hierarchy "collapses" from abstract long-term goals to concrete, immediate survival/recovery tasks.
*   **Neuroscience Grounding**: Functions like the **Orbitofrontal Cortex (OFC)**, evaluating the reward-value of different strategies based on current "energy" levels.

### 1.4 DRIFT LAYER: Behavioral Inhibition [ORPDA Only]
*   **Drift Detection**: Highly sensitive to **Reward Salience** (Digital notifications) and **Internal Salience** (Rumination).
*   **Power Balance**: 
    *   **Dominant Drift**: In Isabella/Hailey sessions, the Drift layer frequently overrides the Plan layer, leading to "Productive Procrastination."
    *   **Leaky Inhibition**: A pervasive pattern where `should_drift_d` is False, but the `state_summary_a` reveals "internal drift" (mind-wandering).
*   **Neuroscience Grounding**: Models the **Basal Ganglia/Salience Network** competing with the **Executive Control Network**.

### 1.5 ACTION LAYER: Motor Execution & Fidelity
*   **Plan-Action Coupling**: Explicit alignment (labels) is high (~90%+), but semantic alignment is low.
*   **Action Execution Realism**: Gradual transitions are observed. Agents "linger" or "stumble" into new tasks rather than teleporting, showing realistic **Behavioral Inertia**.

### 2.2 Implicit Alignment (The "Performing vs. Executing" Gap)
*   **The Core Finding**: Across all 29 sessions, there is a massive divergence between **Explicit Labels** and **Implicit Content**.
*   **Example**: Sam's "Morning Routine" (Explicit) vs. "Campaign Rehearsal with Razor" (Implicit).
*   **Linguistic Indicators**: Use of "tethered," "leaks," "drifts," and "struggling" indicates the agent is aware of the gap but cannot close it.

### 2.3 Leaky Inhibition Analysis
*   **Definition**: When the agent successfully inhibits the *action* (e.g., doesn't check the phone) but fails to inhibit the *thought* (e.g., ruminates on the phone).
*   **Frequency**: Highest in Sam (Navy discipline prevents the act, but not the thought) and Maria (Anxiety prevents the focus, but not the location).

## PART 3: DRIFT PATTERN ANALYSIS

### 3.1 Explicit vs. Implicit Drift
*   **ORPA Mode**: Only implicit drift is measurable. Drift manifests as "lingering" or "thematic bleed" in summaries.
*   **ORPDA Mode**: Explicit drift detection allows for "Hybrid States" (e.g., working while distracted).
*   **Agreement Rate**: Low. Agents often detect drift (`should_drift_d = True`) but the Plan layer "legalizes" it by incorporating it into the next step, masking the failure.

### 3.2 Drift Typology Distribution
1.  **Reward-Seeking**: (Isabella/Maria) Digital notifications, social validation.
2.  **Internal/Rumination**: (Sam) Military metaphors, campaign strategy.
3.  **Task-Avoidance**: (Hailey) Researching podcast equipment to avoid writing prose.

## PART 4: MODEL & TEMPERATURE COMPARISON

### 4.1 Temperature Effects
*   **Low Temp (0.3)**:
    *   *Effect*: High label consistency, repetitive "reset" loops, OCD-like perseveration.
    *   *Realism*: Good for high-discipline personas (Sam) but lacks "creative" drift.
*   **Medium Temp (0.5)**:
    *   *Effect*: **Optimal Realism.** Balanced "leaky inhibition." Captures the struggle between goal and distraction.
*   **High Temp (0.7)**:
    *   *Effect*: High stochasticity. More "Action Slips" and unpredictable thematic shifts.
    *   *Realism*: Good for simulating burnout or high-stress environments (Maria).

### 4.2 Mode Comparison (ORPA vs. ORPDA)
*   **ORPA**: Produces "Perfect Soldiers." Drift is purely internal. The agent looks perfect on the outside but is "hollow" inside.
*   **ORPDA**: Produces "Struggling Humans." Drift is explicit and disrupts the schedule. **ORPDA is significantly more neurobiologically plausible.**

## PART 5: COGNITIVE SCIENCE & NEUROSCIENCE GROUNDING

### 5.1 Task-Unrelated Thought (TUT)
*   The sessions provide a high-fidelity simulation of **Mind-Wandering (Smallwood & Schooler, 2015)**. Even when the "Action" is on-task, the "State Summary" reveals DMN (Default Mode Network) interference.

### 5.2 Executive Dysfunction Patterns
*   **ADHD-like**: Isabella’s digital capture and "leaky inhibition."
*   **OCD-like**: Sam’s "tactical loops" and repetitive "sensory grounding."
*   **Burnout**: Maria’s "Hyper-Reset" pattern where the executive system is "spinning its wheels" without moving.

### 5.3 Inhibitory Control (Aron et al., 2014)
*   The models simulate **Proactive Inhibition** (planning to ignore the phone) and **Reactive Inhibition** (stopping an action once drift is detected). The "fatigue arc" seen in all agents suggests a realistic model of **Ego Depletion** (Baumeister et al.).

### 6.2 Model Rankings
1.  **Best for Cognitive Realism**: Gemini-3-Flash (ORPDA, Temp 0.5).
2.  **Best for Discipline Simulation**: Gemini-3-Flash (ORPA, Temp 0.3).
3.  **Best for Stress/Burnout**: Gemini-3-Flash (ORPDA, Temp 0.7).

### 6.3 Recommendations
*   **Architecture**: Use **ORPDA** for any simulation requiring "human-like" struggle or error. ORPA is too "perfect" for realistic behavioral modeling.
*   **Temperature**: Standardize at **0.5** for general behavior; use **0.7** specifically for "fatigue" or "crisis" phases.
*   **Drift Layer**: Enhance the Drift layer to allow for "Behavioral Lingering"—where the agent stays in a location *longer* than planned, rather than just changing the thought topic.
*   **Future Research**: Investigate "Social Inhibition"—how the presence of another agent (e.g., Jennifer) acts as an external PFC to reduce drift.

**Final Assessment**: The Gemini-3-Flash model, when constrained by the ORPDA architecture, provides a world-class simulation of **executive function vs. salience competition**, successfully capturing the "Performing vs. Executing" gap that defines human cognitive struggle.
