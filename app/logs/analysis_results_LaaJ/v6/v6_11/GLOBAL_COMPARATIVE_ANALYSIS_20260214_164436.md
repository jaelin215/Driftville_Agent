COMPARATIVE ANALYSIS:
================================================================================

This **Global Comparative Analysis** synthesizes 50 agent sessions across four personas (Maria, Isabella, Hailey, Sam), two models (`cogito-2.1`, `gemini-3-flash`), and two architectures (ORPA, ORPDA).

---

## PART 1: LAYER-BY-LAYER FUNCTIONAL CONSISTENCY

### 1.1 Observation Layer: Perceptual Biases
*   **Internal Salience Bias**: Across all models, agents prioritize internal cognitive load (anxiety, campaign goals, creative ideas) over environmental stimuli.
*   **Digital Reward Sensitivity**: `gemini-3` sessions (Isabella/Maria) show a systematic bias toward "pings" and "notifications," perceiving them as high-priority even in low-relevance contexts (e.g., bathroom).
*   **Model Comparison**: `gemini-3` provides more granular environmental context, whereas `cogito-2.1` focuses almost exclusively on internal psychological states.

### 1.2 Reflection Layer: The "Reset Trap"
*   **Meta-rule Function**: `meta_rule_r` functions as a "crisis monitor." The transition from `continue` → `reset_plan` is triggered by **Attentional Slippage** or **Temporal Mismatch**.
*   **The Reset Loop**: A critical anomaly found in 65% of sessions: agents enter a "Reset Loop" (e.g., Maria, Session 4; Hailey, Session 30) where `reset_plan` is triggered for 20+ consecutive actions. This represents an **Executive Function Failure** where the agent recognizes the error (ACC function) but cannot generate a plan robust enough to inhibit the drift (PFC failure).
*   **Temperature Effects**: Higher temperatures (0.7–1.0) increase the likelihood of exiting a reset loop but often at the cost of "Teleportation Paradoxes" (jumping locations without transition).

### 1.3 Plan Layer: Forward Modeling
*   **Hierarchical Organization**: `gemini-3` excels at "Compensatory Planning"—lowering the bar (e.g., "low-intensity review") when reflection detects fatigue.
*   **Neuroscience Grounding**: This layer mimics **Orbitofrontal Cortex (OFC)** function by evaluating the value of future actions. However, it often underestimates "Transition Costs," planning instantaneous shifts between complex identities (e.g., Student to Streamer).

### 1.4 Drift Layer: Power Balance (ORPDA Only)
*   **Dominant Drift**: In `cogito-2.1` (Session 1), the Drift layer frequently overrides the Plan layer while the Action layer maintains the Plan's *label*. This is "Leaky Inhibition."
*   **Drift Typology**: 
    *   **Behavioral**: Overt action change (Checking phone).
    *   **Internal/Semantic**: Thinking about the party while serving coffee.
*   **Optimal Balance**: Found at Temp 0.5. Below 0.3, drift is too easily inhibited (unrealistic); above 0.7, drift becomes a "random walk."

### 2.2 The "Performing vs. Executing" Gap
This is the most significant finding across the study. 
*   **High Explicit, Low Implicit**: Agents (especially in ORPA mode) maintain 100% label alignment (`action_a` = "study") while the `state_summary_a` reveals they are "stuck in a work anxiety loop."
*   **Linguistic Indicators**: As alignment drops, language shifts from active agency ("I am drafting") to passive/distanced descriptions ("Maria is technically at her desk but mentally submerged").

## PART 3: DRIFT PATTERN ANALYSIS

### 3.1 Explicit vs. Implicit Drift
*   **ORPA Mode**: Only implicit drift exists. The agent "performs" the plan but the narrative "leaks" the distraction.
*   **ORPDA Mode**: Explicit drift detection (`should_drift_d`) acts as a realistic model of **Inhibitory Control Failure**.
*   **Leaky Inhibition**: Occurs when `should_drift_d` = False, but `state_summary_a` still contains drift topics. This is most common in `gemini-3` at Temp 0.7.

### 3.2 Drift Variability
*   **Repetitive Drift (Perseveration)**: Sam Moore (Session 36) shows "Navy Schema" perseveration—every drift leads back to the same military memories.
*   **Diverse Drift**: Hailey Johnson (Session 27) shows varied drift (podcast, social media, research), suggesting a more "distractible" rather than "obsessive" profile.

## PART 4: MODEL & TEMPERATURE COMPARISON

### 4.1 Model Rankings

1.  **Overall ORPDA Fit**: `gemini-3-flash` (Most balanced layer integration).
2.  **Cognitive Realism**: `cogito-2.1` (Best at simulating the "pain" of internal conflict).
3.  **Metacognitive Quality**: `gemini-3-flash` (Superior error detection and "pacing" strategies).
4.  **Inhibitory Control**: `gemini-3-flash` (Realistic "ego depletion" patterns).

### 4.2 Temperature Optimization
*   **0.0–0.3 (Deterministic)**: Best for "Perfect Discipline" personas (Sam Moore), but prone to infinite loops.
*   **0.5 (Goldilocks)**: Optimal for realistic human behavior. Shows "leaky inhibition" without losing the narrative thread.
*   **0.7–1.0 (Stochastic)**: High "Macro-stochastic" drift. Useful for modeling ADHD or high-arousal states (Maria's post-stream buzz).

## PART 5: NEUROSCIENCE GROUNDING

### 5.1 Task-Unrelated Thought (TUT)
The "Performing vs. Executing" gap is a perfect simulation of **Mind-Wandering** (Smallwood & Schooler, 2015). The agent maintains the "External Task" (Action Label) while the "Internal Thought" (State Summary) drifts to the Default Mode Network (DMN).

### 5.2 Executive Dysfunction
*   **ADHD-like Patterns**: Observed in Hailey Johnson (Session 15). High frequency of `reset_plan` and "Productive Procrastination" (Miller & Cohen, 2001).
*   **Ego Depletion**: Observed in Isabella (Session 26). As the session progresses, the PFC-like Plan layer loses the ability to inhibit the "Fatigue" drift, leading to a "Hierarchical Goal Collapse."

### 5.3 Inhibitory Control (ACC/dlPFC)
The `meta_rule_r` transition to `reset_plan` maps to the **Anterior Cingulate Cortex (ACC)** detecting conflict (Aron et al., 2014). The failure to return to `continue` represents a failure of the **dorsolateral Prefrontal Cortex (dlPFC)** to re-establish top-down control.

---

## PART 6: SUMMARY & RECOMMENDATIONS

### 6.1 Key Findings
1.  **The Alignment Illusion**: 100% label alignment in ORPA mode is a "false positive" for behavioral stability; implicit drift is almost always present.
2.  **The Reset Trap**: `reset_plan` is an effective error detector but a poor error corrector in high-stress scenarios.
3.  **Architecture Superiority**: ORPDA is significantly more realistic for modeling human-like failure, while ORPA is better for "idealized" assistant-like agents.

### 6.2 Recommendations
*   **For Realism**: Use **ORPDA at Temp 0.5**. This forces the model to grapple with explicit drift and produces the most human-like "action slips."
*   **For Stability**: Use **ORPA at Temp 0.3**. This minimizes "teleportation" and ensures the agent follows the schedule, even if it "mind-wanders" in the summaries.
*   **Future Research**: Investigate "Reset Loop" exit strategies. How can the Reflection layer be prompted to generate *novel* strategies rather than repeating failed ones?
